# turn-003

## Question

Looking only at FY2024 and FY2025, does Microsoft's consolidated operating leverage support a stronger profitability outlook, or would that conclusion overstate what the results show?

## Answer

Conclusion: Microsoft's consolidated operating leverage does not strongly support a stronger profitability outlook based on the FY2024 and FY2025 data.

Drivers: The incremental operating margin of 52.17% from FY2024 to FY2025 is a significant increase, but it is largely driven by revenue growth rather than operating efficiency improvements. The operating margin increased by 97.8 basis points, which is a moderate increase.

Quantifications:

| Fiscal year (exact period) | Revenue (USD millions) | Operating Income (USD millions) | Operating margin |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| FY2024 (2023-07-01 to 2024-06-30) | 245,122.000 | 109,433.000 | 44.64% |
| FY2025 (2024-07-01 to 2025-06-30) | 281,724.000 | 128,528.000 | 45.62% |

| Δ | FY2024 | FY2025 | Δ/Δ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue | 36,602.000 | 281,724.000 |  |
| Operating Income | 19,095.000 | 128,528.000 | 52.17% |
| Operating Margin | 97.8 basis points |  |  |

Calculations:

- Revenue growth: 281,724.000 - 245,122.000 = 36,602.000 million
- Operating income growth: 128,528.000 - 109,433.000 = 19,095.000 million
- Incremental operating margin: (19,095.000 / 36,602.000) × 100 = 52.17%

The increase in operating margin is largely driven by revenue growth, which may not necessarily translate to sustained profitability improvements. The operating leverage is not strongly supported by the data, and further analysis is needed to determine the underlying drivers of the increase in operating margin.

Limitation: The analysis is limited by the lack of data on operating expenses, capital expenditures, and other factors that may influence operating leverage.
