# turn-005

## Question

Looking only at FY2024 and FY2025, does Microsoft's consolidated operating leverage support a stronger profitability outlook, or would that conclusion overstate what the results show?

## Answer

Conclusion: Microsoft's consolidated operating leverage does not strongly support a stronger profitability outlook for FY2026 and beyond. The incremental operating margin of 52.17% is largely driven by revenue growth, and the operating margin increase of 97.8 basis points is not sufficient to establish a strong profitability outlook.

Drivers: The significant revenue growth in FY2025 ($36,602 million) is the primary driver of the operating margin increase. This growth is largely due to the company's strong cloud computing business, which has been a key contributor to its revenue growth.

Quantifications:

| Fiscal year (exact period) | Revenue (USD millions) | Operating Income (USD millions) | Operating margin |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| FY2024 (2023-07-01 to 2024-06-30) | 245,122.000 | 109,433.000 | 44.64% |
| FY2025 (2024-07-01 to 2025-06-30) | 281,724.000 | 128,528.000 | 45.62% |

| Δ | FY2024 | FY2025 | Δ/Δ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Revenue | 36,602.000 | 281,724.000 | 52.17% |
| Operating Income | 19,095.000 | 128,528.000 | 672.19% |
| Operating Margin | 97.8 basis points | 45.62% |  |

Calculations:

- Revenue growth: 281,724,000 - 245,122,000 = 36,602,000
- Operating income growth: 128,528,000 - 109,433,000 = 19,095,000
- Incremental operating margin: (19,095,000 / 36,602,000) × 100 = 52.17%

Limitations: This analysis only considers the reported-period calculations and does not establish the cause or future persistence of the change. Additionally, the operating margin increase is largely driven by revenue growth, which may not be sustainable in the future.
