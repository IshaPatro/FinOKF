## Conclusion
Microsoft's FY2024→FY2025 consolidated results show modest, genuine operating leverage — margin expanded ~98bps to 45.62%, with incremental margin (52.17%) exceeding the base-year margin (44.64%). This supports a moderately stronger profitability outlook, but a single year-over-year data point cannot establish a durable trend or its cause.

## What the data shows
- Operating margin improved from 44.64% to 45.62%, a +97.8bps gain — a real but incremental change, not a step-function shift.
- Incremental margin of 52.17% (Δoperating income/Δrevenue) exceeding the average margin indicates that revenue added in FY2025 converted to operating income more efficiently than the existing base, consistent with positive operating leverage.
- This is consolidated data only; it aggregates all segments (Productivity, Intelligent Cloud, More Personal Computing) and cannot isolate which businesses or cost lines drove the leverage.

## What it cannot establish
- Two fiscal years is too short a window to confirm a repeatable trend versus a one-off effect (e.g., mix shift, cost timing, or nonrecurring items).
- Consolidated margin changes do not reveal segment-level drivers — cloud/AI infrastructure investment, capex intensity, or opex discipline could each be masking offsetting movements.
- No cash-flow, capex, or segment data were reviewed here, so this cannot confirm cash conversion quality or whether margin gains are capex-funded and sustainable.
