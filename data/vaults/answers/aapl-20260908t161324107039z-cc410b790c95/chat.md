**Conclusion:** Apple retained more of each revenue dollar after direct costs in both years, but the pace of improvement decelerated sharply — gross margin expansion nearly a third as fast in FY2025 versus FY2024.

**Detail:** Gross margin rose from 44.13% (FY2023) to 46.21% (FY2024), a +207.5 bp gain, then to 46.91% (FY2025), a further +69.9 bp gain. |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| 2025 | 416,161 | 220,960 | 46.91% | +69.9 |

**What this shows vs. what it cannot establish:** The trend confirms Apple is capturing a larger share of revenue as gross profit and that improvement continued into FY2025, but at a materially weaker pace. This margin-only view cannot identify whether the deceleration stems from mix shift, input costs, pricing, or component of revenue (products vs. services) — that requires segment/COGS-line detail not present here.

How would you characterize Apple's consolidated gross margin performance in FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025: steady progress or an uneven improvement? Explain its significance for an analyst and the limits of extrapolating the trend.

## Finokf · Turn 2

## Apple Consolidated Gross Margin: Uneven Improvement, Decelerating

Apple's gross margin **improved each year but at a sharply decelerating pace** — this is progress, not steady, linear improvement.

| Fiscal Year | Revenue ($M) | COGS ($M) | Gross Margin | YoY Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2023 | 383,285 | 214,137 | 44.13% | — |
| FY2024 | 391,035 | 210,352 | 46.21% | +207.5 bps |
| FY2025 | 416,161 | 220,960 | 46.91% | +69.9 bps |

**Significance for an analyst:** The FY2023→FY2024 jump (+207.5 bps) was large, likely reflecting mix shift, pricing, or cost efficiencies; the FY2024→FY2025 gain (+69.9 bps) is roughly one-third the size, despite revenue growing faster ($25.1B vs $7.8B added). This signals **margin expansion is losing momentum** even as top-line growth accelerates — worth flagging for valuation models that assume continued multi-hundred-bps annual gains.

**Limits of extrapolation:**
- Two data points of deceleration do not establish a trend line; FY2026 could reaccelerate, plateau, or reverse.
- Consolidated margin masks segment mix (Products vs. Services) and geographic/FX effects not shown here.
- No cost-driver detail (component costs, forex, mix) is available to explain *why* growth slowed — correlation only, not causation.
- Single-year COGS/revenue ratios don't capture quarterly volatility within each fiscal year.

**Conclusion:** Directionally positive but uneven — analysts should model margin scenarios rather than assume a fixed bps/year cadence.

Did Apple's consolidated gross margin change between FY2024 and FY2025 materially improve the economics of each revenue dollar? What does that comparison tell an investor, and what does it leave unresolved?

## Finokf · Turn 3

## Conclusion
Apple's consolidated gross margin improved modestly, rising **69.9 basis points** from 46.21% (FY2024) to 46.91% (FY2025). This is a real but incremental improvement in revenue-dollar economics, not a step-change.

## What it tells an investor
- Each dollar of revenue retained slightly more gross profit: gross profit grew **6.9%** (to $195.2B from $180.7B) while revenue grew **6.4%** ($416.2B vs $391.0B), so margin expansion added a small incremental lift beyond pure volume growth.
- The consistency of a ~46-47% gross margin band signals pricing power and cost discipline are stable, not deteriorating — a modest positive signal on unit economics.

## What it leaves unresolved
- **No segment/product mix disclosed here**: the blended margin move could reflect Services (typically higher-margin) growing faster than Products, favorable component costs, FX, or a shift in unit mix — this data cannot isolate the driver.
- **No forward view**: 70bps is within normal year-to-year variability and does not establish a trend or its persistence into FY2026.
- **Below gross profit is unaddressed**: this comparison says nothing about opex leverage, R&D/SG&A trends, operating margin, or net income conversion — a full profitability picture requires those figures.
- **Cash conversion untested**: gross margin is an accrual accounting metric: it doesn't confirm whether the incremental profit converted to cash flow (CFO vs. net income).

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue ($M) | 391,035 | 416,161 | +6.4% |
| COGS ($M) | 210,352 | 220,960 | +5.0% |
| Gross Margin | 46.21% | 46.91% | +69.9 bps |
