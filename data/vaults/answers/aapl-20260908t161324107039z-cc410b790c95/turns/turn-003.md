# turn-003

## Question

Did Apple's consolidated gross margin change between FY2024 and FY2025 materially improve the economics of each revenue dollar? What does that comparison tell an investor, and what does it leave unresolved?

## Answer

## Conclusion
Apple's consolidated gross margin improved modestly, rising **69.9 basis points** from 46.21% (FY2024) to 46.91% (FY2025). This is a real but incremental improvement in revenue-dollar economics, not a step-change.

## What it tells an investor
- Each dollar of revenue retained slightly more gross profit: gross profit grew **6.9%** (to $195.2B from $180.7B) while revenue grew **6.4%** ($416.2B vs $391.0B), so margin expansion added a small incremental lift beyond pure volume growth.
- The consistency of a ~46-47% gross margin band signals pricing power and cost discipline are stable, not deteriorating — a modest positive signal on unit economics.

## What it leaves unresolved
- **No segment/product mix disclosed here**: the blended margin move could reflect Services (typically higher-margin) growing faster than Products, favorable component costs, FX, or a shift in unit mix — this data cannot isolate the driver.
- **No forward view**: 70bps is within normal year-to-year variability and does not establish a trend or its persistence into FY2026.
- **Below gross profit is unaddressed**: this comparison says nothing about opex leverage, R&D/SG&A trends, operating margin, or net income conversion — a full profitability picture requires those figures.
- **Cash conversion untested**: gross margin is an accrual accounting metric: it doesn't confirm whether the incremental profit converted to cash flow (CFO vs. net income).

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue ($M) | 391,035 | 416,161 | +6.4% |
| COGS ($M) | 210,352 | 220,960 | +5.0% |
| Gross Margin | 46.21% | 46.91% | +69.9 bps |
